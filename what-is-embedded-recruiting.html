What is embedded recruiting?

Embedded recruiting is a model where an experienced recruiter works as part of your hiring team, across your open roles, for a set period or level of support. Instead of working each role from the outside for a placement fee, the recruiter works from the inside, representing your company to candidates as an extension of your team.

How embedded recruiting works

An embedded recruiter starts the way a strong internal recruiter would: by learning your business, your hiring managers, and the roles you need to fill. From there, they run full-cycle recruiting on your openings, from sourcing and screening through scheduling, candidate communication, and support through offer.

The key difference from an agency is who they represent. An embedded recruiter speaks for your company and your employer brand, works multiple roles in parallel, and prioritizes alongside your hiring managers based on what the business needs most. When the engagement ends, the pipelines, candidate relationships, and notes stay with you.

Embedded recruiting vs. an agency

A traditional agency works externally on a specific role and typically earns a placement fee for each hire, often a meaningful percentage of the new hire's first-year salary. That works well for a single hard-to-fill role. It gets expensive fast when you're making several hires, because you pay that fee again on every one.

Embedded recruiting flips the cost model. You pay for recruiting capacity, usually as a predictable engagement cost, and that capacity works across as many roles as it can carry. The more you're hiring, the stronger the economics.

Embedded recruiting vs. RPO

Embedded recruiting is often confused with RPO (recruitment process outsourcing). RPO typically hands an outside provider responsibility for all or a large part of your hiring, usually under a longer-term contract. Embedded recruiting is lighter and more flexible: a recruiter joins your team to add capacity and expertise, and the engagement scales up during a hiring push and back down as roles close.

When embedded recruiting is the right fit

  • You're making several hires and don't have the recruiting capacity to keep up
  • You have an internal recruiter, but a hiring surge is more than one person can carry
  • Agency fees across multiple hires have become hard to justify
  • You need experienced recruiting support but aren't ready to add permanent recruiting headcount
  • You want the candidate experience to feel like your company, not an outside firm

When it isn't

If you have one or two difficult roles and no broader hiring push, a contingent search is usually the better fit: you pay only when the role is filled. And if hiring keeps breaking because there's no real process, technology, or structure behind it, the problem may be infrastructure rather than capacity. That's where building your talent acquisition function, often with fractional leadership, solves more than another recruiter would.

What to look for in an embedded recruiting partner

The best embedded recruiters do more than fill requisitions. Look for someone with real talent acquisition experience who can spot problems with intake, process, sourcing, candidate experience, or hiring-manager alignment while they're doing the recruiting work. That way you fill the roles today and hire better tomorrow.

Experience in your talent market matters too. Recruiting civil and structural engineers, for example, takes a different network and a different pitch than recruiting software developers.

Common questions

Is embedded recruiting the same as RPO?

They're related but not the same. RPO typically hands an outside provider responsibility for all or a large part of your hiring, usually under a long-term contract. Embedded recruiting is lighter and more flexible: a recruiter joins your team to add capacity and expertise, and the engagement scales up or down with your hiring.

How much does embedded recruiting cost?

Embedded recruiting is usually priced as a predictable engagement cost, such as a monthly or hourly arrangement, rather than a percentage of each hire's salary. For companies making several hires, that often costs less than paying an agency fee on every placement.

How long does an embedded recruiting engagement last?

It depends on your hiring. Some engagements cover a specific hiring push of a few months; others continue as ongoing, part-time support. The model is designed to flex as your needs change.
